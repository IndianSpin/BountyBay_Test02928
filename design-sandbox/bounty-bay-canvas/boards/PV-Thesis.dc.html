PV0 · A — Product experience thesis

Outnegotiate real people.

Bounty Bay is not a negotiation simulator. It is a competitive social game in which the skill being contested is negotiation. The repo already says so: human PvP is canonical, and “players create the negotiation” (docs/00). This pass makes the product behave like it.

Two people, one number, two secrets. Every move is a claim about yourself, and the rules already put a price on each kind of claim. Talk is free, so it can lie. A concession costs chips and can never be taken back, so it can't. Waiting costs your bounty, and the other player watches it drain. Reading someone is comparing what they say with what they pay for. The game's job is to put those two things side by side and then get out of the way.

Then the cards turn over. The showdown tells you whether your theory about the person was right, and that is the moment that makes you demand a rematch. Swapping sides removes the last excuse (“I had the easy role”). A rival turns a stranger into unfinished business.

What changes: the person across the table becomes the largest thing on screen. Every animation amplifies something a human did. The interface shrinks to what the current state needs. And every match ends as a story about someone, not a list of numbers.

The story test, run on PV2

“She opened at 120 and said she had another buyer at 110 — then dropped 24 in four seconds. I held and made her watch my clock. Her ‘buyer’ went from 110 to 80. I showed her the re-cut bezel and she took 67. Her minimum was 49. I left 18 on the table.”

PASSES  It is about a person, a claim, a read and a mistake.

Fails: “I started at 55, moved to 58, 62, 67 and settled at 67.”

Nothing new is needed to make bluffing meaningful. Words are cheap; moves and time are not. The design has to make both visible at the same moment.

One rule for characters: the avatar performs; it never feels on your behalf

Allowed on a human's avatar: what the human did (offer, accept, reveal, walk), what they chose to express (quick lines, emotes, pitches), and true public states (thinking = their clock is running; listening = it is not their turn).

Never: reactions the system invents about my move on their avatar — irritation, surprise, “close to a deal” excitement. On a real person those are fake tells, and players would learn to read them. AI opponents may react freely because they are labelled AI throughout (GR-020).

Bluffs get no system response at all. The game never marks, hints at or animates the truth of anything said.
